📘 Equations & origin story
Boids, the golden ratio, the logistic map, and V formation flight
A richer built-in guide for the math, the creator, and the layered design of this game build.
Who created Boids, and did Reynolds name it?
Yes. The term comes from Craig Reynolds himself.
Craig W. Reynolds created the model in 1986 and published the famous SIGGRAPH paper in 1987. In that paper he explicitly says the simulated bird-like “bird-oid” objects are referred to generically as “boids”, even when they stand in for other creatures such as schooling fish. So the name is Reynolds’ own phrasing, not a later nickname.
creator: Craig W. Reynolds
model created: 1986
paper published: SIGGRAPH 1987
name origin: “bird-oid” → boid
A little about Craig Reynolds
Reynolds studied computer science at MIT, later built a career around procedural animation, autonomous characters, flocking, steering behaviours, crowds, and game technology, and spent many years in research and production work. His public résumé lists MIT degrees, film/game animation experience, Sony Computer Entertainment research, later work on simulation for autonomous vehicles, and an Academy Scientific and Technical Award for pioneering work in 3D computer animation. His current site says he retired in 2020 and continues as an unaffiliated researcher.
Where Boids showed up in animation and film
Reynolds’ own Boids page says the first animation built directly around the model was Stanley and Stella in: Breaking the Ice (1987). He also says the first feature-film use was Tim Burton’s Batman Returns (1992), where modified Boids software helped drive bat swarms and penguin flock motion. The same page also points to Disney’s The Lion King (1994) for the wildebeest stampede as part of the broader flocking / group-motion lineage. Reynolds’ history page also mentions other procedural group-motion examples around Menagerie and Star Trek: Voyager’s Elogium in the wider behavioral-animation family.
Core Boids rule: separation
F_{separation} = \sum rac{p_i - p_j}{\lVert p_i - p_j Vert^2}
Push away from nearby neighbours so birds do not overlap.
Core Boids rule: alignment
F_{alignment} = \overline{v}_{local} - v_i
Turn toward the local average heading.
Core Boids rule: cohesion
F_{cohesion} = \hat{c} \; \min(\max(0, d - d_{comfort}), d_{cap}) \; w_c
This build now uses a softened cohesion pull rather than a raw centroid yank, which helps stop the tiny-circle bug you noticed.
Steering blend used by this game
a_i = w_s F_s + w_a F_a + w_c F_c + F_{edge} + F_{wind} + F_{golden} + F_{logistic} + F_{formation}
Classic flocking plus optional layered add-ons.
Golden ratio
arphi = rac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} pprox 1.6180339887
rac{1}{ arphi} = arphi - 1 pprox 0.6180339887
arphi^2 = arphi + 1
In this game φ is not treated as a hard law of bird motion. It is used as a stylistic shaping idea for turn feel and speed shimmer.
How golden-ratio mode is used here
goldenWave = \sin( arphi t + s) + 0.65\cos((1 + 1/ arphi)t + s\sqrt{5})
F_{golden} \propto goldenWave \cdot goldenTurnBias
This gives level 2 a φ-flavoured motion layer without yet turning each bird into its own full personality profile.
Logistic map
x_{n+1} = r x_n (1 - x_n)
0 \le x_n \le 1, \; 0 \le r \le 4
Robert May’s 1976 review popularized this map as a vivid example of how very simple nonlinear equations can generate surprisingly rich and even chaotic behaviour.
How logistic-map personality works here
x_{bird} = f^{(k)}(x_0, r)
personalityOffset = (x_{bird} - 0.5) imes 2 imes strength
trait = baseTrait imes (1 + personalityOffset imes span)
Each bird gets its own seed, which is pushed through the logistic map to create a signed individuality offset. That offset then slightly perturbs awareness, spacing, steering, speed, wing feel, and formation preference.
V formation layer
slotTarget = leaderPos - \hat{v}_{leader}\cos( heta) d + \hat{n}_{leader}\sin( heta) d
F_{slot} \propto (slotTarget - p_i)
Level 4 assigns left/right slots behind a leader and pulls birds toward those aerodynamic positions.
Wake avoidance
F_{wake} \propto side imes wakeAvoidanceStrength
Birds are nudged away from the dead wake directly behind the leader, encouraging those diagonal V offsets instead of a straight line.
How the four levels now fit together
Level 1 is pure Boids. Level 2 adds golden-ratio styling only. Level 3 adds logistic-map personality, which is where each bird starts to feel more unique. Level 4 adds the V-formation decision layer on top. That means the ideas stack cleanly instead of all being mixed together too early.
